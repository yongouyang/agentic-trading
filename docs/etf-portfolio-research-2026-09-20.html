15. Should every portfolio carry a VOO sleeve as defense? (2026-09-21)

Short answer from the data: no — VOO is diluted offense, not defense. ~35–40% of the S&P 500 is now the same mega-cap AI trade that drives SMH/QQQM (daily corr 0.90 to QQQM, 0.80 to SMH over 5Y), so in the one scenario that matters (§14 AI-capex digestion) VOO falls nearly as hard as the portfolio it's supposed to protect.

  • 20% VOO buys 1–1.5pp of protection (2022: −22.4→−21.5; bear scenario: −42.5→−41.0) and costs return in 61/61 rolling starts (−8pp median terminal return, −3.4pp XIRR). Worst trade in the study.
  • 20% GLD buys 5–9.5pp at lower return cost, and halves the median trough (−8.5%→−3.6%).
  • VOO also finished last or near-last on returns in every table in this document — as a core it's the no-view default; as a defense it fails because its defensive identity (broad diversification) has been hollowed out by mega-cap concentration.
  • What actually defends a growth portfolio: GLD (0.16 corr, +5% in the bear scenario), XLV (−0.16 corr to SMH YTD — FG-B already holds 20%), SCHD. FG-B already contains the growth-universe diversifier; adding VOO would double-count QQQM/SPMO exposure, not hedge it.
  • When VOO is the right answer: if you want the index — not your themes — to decide sector weights (i.e., you no longer endorse the AI-concentration thesis), or for behavioral simplicity. That's a different portfolio decision, not a sleeve decision.
  • Under the user's stated premise (drawdown secondary), the honest logic is: defense costs terminal return; buy it only with assets that actually diversify (GLD/XLV/SCHD), and only in proportion to your doubt about the thesis or your future discipline — not as a ritual VOO slice.

Bottom line

If one line has to survive contact with the future: 5×20 — VOO 20 / SMH 20 / SPMO 20 / GLD 20 / SCHD 20, rebalanced quarterly or at ±10% drift. It keeps ~70% of the all-growth upside in hot years, cuts the 2022-style loss from −45% to −13%, needs no forecasts, and every sleeve is a distinct economic mechanism. QLD, SOXL, and WQTM don't make the cut on any window longer than a year; QQQM is redundant against VOO+SMH. (Under the user's stated premise — no leverage, no sell-out pressure, drawdown secondary — see §13–14: FG-B "full-stack" supersedes this pick.)
