Trade Simulation & Transaction Costs

The strategy clearly makes money — so why might live trading lose money? (策略明明赚钱，为什么实盘可能亏钱？) Real trading must simulate slippage and commissions. Without a Broker, backtesting is just pretending to trade!

Source: knowledge-base/day_20 (IMG_6631–IMG_6638), "30天学习量化投资 · Day 20: 成交模拟与交易成本" · Extracted 2025-08-30

1Strategy Thinks, Broker Executes

Strategy produces ideas. Broker simulates real fills with slippage, commissions, and partial fills.

Strategy (策略) — the researcher

  • Analyzes market data and indicators
  • Generates signals based on rules
  • Decides "want to buy" or "want to sell"
  • Does NOT handle funds, positions, costs, or fills

Broker (执行) — the trader

  • Receives orders
  • Determines fill price (with slippage)
  • Calculates commission fees
  • Simulates partial fills
  • Outputs Trade records
Key insight: Backtesting without a Broker = pretending to trade! Only by considering slippage and commissions can backtest results approach real trading performance. (没有Broker的回测 = 假装交易！)

2Ideal Fill vs Real Fill

Backtesting can't simply assume "signal price = fill price". In real trading, the price you see and the price you get are always different.

Ideal fill (common backtest assumption)

  • Signal price = Fill price
  • No slippage
  • No commission
  • Always fully filled
  • Result: overly optimistic

Real fill (needs simulation)

  • Signal price ≠ Fill price
  • Buy more expensive (slippage)
  • Sell cheaper (slippage)
  • Commission on every trade
  • Result: costs erode profits, may turn profitable → losing
Real fill cost= Signal price+ Slippage+ Commission+ Other costs

3Slippage — The Price Gap

Slippage = actual fill price − expected price. You buy more expensive and sell cheaper than you planned.

costSlippage on BUY vs SELL

BUY slippage (buy more expensive)

Expected price10.00
Actual fill10.02
Slippage+0.02 (买贵了)

SELL slippage (sell cheaper)

Expected price10.00
Actual fill9.98
Slippage−0.02 (卖便宜了)

Why does slippage happen?

Low liquidity

Insufficient bid/ask depth — large orders push prices.

High volatility

Prices change rapidly between order and fill.

Large order size

Your order eats through multiple price levels.

Network latency

Time gap between signal and market arrival.

Gap (跳空)

Opening price skips past your intended price.

Cumulative slippage impact

💡 0.02 per trade seems small — but with frequent trading, the cumulative cost becomes enormous. If each trade's gross profit is only 0.05, slippage alone can turn it into a loss. (0.02元好像不多？但交易很多次后，累计起来非常可观！)

4Commission — The Cost of Every Trade

Commission = fill amount × rate (+ other rules). Every trade incurs costs that directly reduce returns.

costCommission formula

Commission (手续费)= Fill amount (成交金额)× Rate (费率)

6Why Transaction Costs Destroy Strategies

A single cost seems small, but high turnover compounds relentlessly, eroding your returns. Alpha must exceed costs for the strategy to survive.

dangerCost erosion example

High turnover makes it worse

Daily turnover (250 trades/year)

Per-trade cost: 0.08%
Annual cost: 0.08% × 250 = 20%

Even a 12% gross return strategy gets completely consumed!

Turnover every 10 days (25 trades/year)

Per-trade cost: 0.08%
Annual cost: 0.08% × 25 = 2%

Strategy's real return space is freed up.

💡 Cost control is a core competitive advantage.
• Strategy optimization: improve win rate, P/L ratio, reduce drawdown
• Trading optimization: reduce turnover, optimize slippage, choose low-fee brokers
• Only when Alpha (strategy edge) > transaction costs can you sustainably profit.

7How to Control Slippage

Slippage can't be eliminated — but it can be reduced through smart execution.

Use limit orders

Set max buy price / min sell price to avoid worst-case fills.

Choose liquid instruments

Active markets with deep order books → smaller slippage.

Avoid volatile periods

Market open/close, major news events → high slippage risk.

Split large orders

Break into smaller chunks to reduce market impact.

Track & analyze

Continuously monitor slippage statistics to optimize execution.
