# Tax Comparison: HK vs US Stocks & ETFs for a Hong Kong Resident

**Date:** 2026-08-30 · **Scope:** Personal tax implications for a Hong Kong
resident trading HK-listed and US-listed stocks and ETFs, including dividend
income, interest, capital gains, and US estate tax exposure. Irish-domiciled
ETFs are included as the recommended alternative.

> ⚠️ **Disclaimer:** This is general information, not tax advice. Consult a
> qualified HK tax advisor for your specific situation, especially regarding
> estate planning and holding structures.

---

## 1. TL;DR

- **Hong Kong has no capital gains tax** and a territorial tax system — only
  HK-sourced income is taxable. This makes HK one of the most tax-efficient
  jurisdictions for stock trading.
- **HK-listed stocks/ETFs are the most tax-efficient:** 0% on capital gains,
  0% dividend withholding, 0% HK income tax on investment income.
- **US-listed stocks/ETFs carry two hidden costs:** a flat **30% US withholding
  tax on dividends** (no HK-US tax treaty to reduce it), and **up to 40% US
  estate tax** on US-situs assets above a mere US$60,000 exemption.
- **Irish-domiciled ETFs are the sweet spot for US market exposure:** dividend
  withholding drops from 30% → 15% (US-Ireland tax treaty), and US estate tax
  risk is eliminated entirely.

## 2. Capital Gains

| | HK Stocks/ETFs | US Stocks/ETFs |
|---|---|---|
| **Tax on Gains** | **None** — HK has no capital gains tax | **None** — HK has no capital gains tax; US does not tax non-resident aliens on capital gains from stocks |

**Verdict:** No capital gains tax in either case for a HK resident (assuming
you are not a US person / green card holder).

## 3. Dividend Income

| | HK Stocks/ETFs | US Stocks/ETFs |
|---|---|---|
| **Source Withholding Tax** | **None** — HK imposes no withholding tax on dividends | **30% withholding tax** by the US IRS (on US-source dividends) |
| **HK Salaries Tax** | Generally **not assessable** unless derived from HK employment/business | Generally **not assessable** under Salaries Tax — investment income is exempt under HK IRO s.8(2) |
| **Effective Tax Rate** | **0%** | **30%** (withheld at source) |

### Key Details on US Withholding Tax

- The US imposes a **flat 30% withholding** on dividends paid to foreign
  (non-US) persons.
- **Hong Kong has no tax treaty with the US**, so you cannot claim a reduced
  rate (unlike, e.g., UK or Canada residents who can claim 15%).
- You must submit **Form W-8BEN** to your broker to certify your non-US status.
  Without it, the broker may apply backup withholding at 30% on gross proceeds
  (not just dividends).
- The 30% is withheld **before** dividends reach your account — you receive
  the net amount. There is no mechanism to reclaim this as a HK resident.

### Worked Example: US Dividend

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross Dividend | US$1,000 |
| US Withholding Tax (30%) | US$300 |
| Net Received | US$700 |
| HK Tax | HK$0 |

### Worked Example: HK Dividend

| Item | Amount |
|---|---|
| Gross Dividend | HK$10,000 |
| Withholding Tax | HK$0 |
| Net Received | HK$10,000 |
| HK Tax | HK$0 |

## 4. Interest Income (e.g., from Bond ETFs)

| | HK Stocks/ETFs | US Stocks/ETFs |
|---|---|---|
| **Source Tax** | None | **0%** — US does not withhold tax on portfolio interest (e.g., from most bond ETFs, treasury interest) |
| **HK Tax** | Not assessable | Not assessable |

**Verdict:** Generally tax-free in both jurisdictions. The US provides a
"portfolio interest exemption" for qualifying interest paid to foreign persons.

## 5. US Estate Tax Exposure ⚠️ (Critical & Often Overlooked)

| | HK Stocks/ETFs | US Stocks/ETFs |
|---|---|---|
| **Estate Tax** | Not applicable | **Up to 40%** on US-situs assets exceeding **US$60,000** |

### Why This Matters

- If you hold **US-situs assets** (US stocks, US-listed ETFs) and pass away,
  the US may levy estate tax on the value of those assets.
- The exemption for non-resident aliens is only **US$60,000** — compare this
  to ~US$13 million for US citizens.
- The tax rate is progressive, reaching **40%** on amounts above US$1 million.
- This applies regardless of where you live or where your will is probated.

### Estate Tax Rate Schedule (Non-Resident Aliens)

| Taxable Amount (US$) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 – 10,000 | 18% |
| 10,001 – 20,000 | 20% |
| 20,001 – 40,000 | 22% |
| 40,001 – 60,000 | 24% |
| 60,001 – 80,000 | 26% |
| 80,001 – 100,000 | 28% |
| 100,001 – 150,000 | 30% |
| 150,001 – 250,000 | 32% |
| 250,001 – 500,000 | 34% |
| 500,001 – 1,000,000 | 37% |
| Over 1,000,000 | 40% |

### What Counts as US-Situs Assets

- US-listed stocks (AAPL, MSFT, etc.) — **yes**
- US-listed ETFs (SPY, VOO, VTI, etc.) — **yes**
- Irish-domiciled ETFs (even if listed on LSE) — **no**
- HK-listed stocks/ETFs — **no**
- Cash in US brokerage accounts — **potentially yes** (consult advisor)

### Mitigation Strategies

1. **Use Irish-domiciled ETFs** — not US-situs, avoids estate tax entirely.
2. **Hold US investments through a non-US holding company** (e.g., BVI or
   Cayman entity) — the company, not you, owns the US assets. Adds complexity
   and cost but effective for large portfolios.
3. **Keep US-situs assets below US$60,000** — impractical for most serious
   investors.

## 6. Irish-Domiciled ETFs: The Recommended Alternative

Irish-domiciled ETFs listed on the London Stock Exchange (LSE) offer a
compelling middle ground for HK residents seeking global/US market exposure.

| Feature | US-Domiciled ETF (e.g., VOO) | Irish-Domiciled ETF (e.g., CSPX.L / VWRA.L) |
|---|---|---|
| **Underlying Index** | S&P 500 | S&P 500 (same exposure) |
| **Dividend Withholding** | 30% | **15%** (US-Ireland tax treaty) |
| **US Estate Tax** | Up to 40% (>$60K) | **None** (not US-situs) |
| **Capital Gains Tax (HK)** | 0% | 0% |
| **HK Income Tax** | 0% | 0% |
| **Currency** | USD | USD (LSE-listed) or GBP |
| **Liquidity** | Very high | Good (lower than US equivalents) |

### Popular Irish-Domiciled ETFs

| Ticker | Name | Index | Accumulating? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CSPX.L** | iShares Core S&P 500 | S&P 500 | Yes (accumulating) |
| **VWRA.L** | Vanguard FTSE All-World | Global (FTSE All-World) | Yes (accumulating) |
| **VUSA.L** | Vanguard S&P 500 | S&P 500 | No (distributing) |
| **ISAC.L** | iShares MSCI ACWI | Global (MSCI ACWI) | Yes (accumulating) |
| **EIMI.L** | iShares Core MSCI EM | Emerging Markets | Yes (accumulating) |

### Accumulating vs Distributing

- **Accumulating ETFs** automatically reinvest dividends inside the fund.
  Since no dividend is paid out to you, there is **no withholding tax event**
  at the investor level — the fund handles US withholding internally at the
  treaty rate (15%). This is the most tax-efficient option.
- **Distributing ETFs** pay dividends to you. The fund still withholds at 15%
  internally, but you receive the net dividend. Slightly less efficient due
  to cash drag and reinvestment costs.

## 8. Action Items for Decision-Making

1. **Default to HK-listed stocks/ETFs** for any HK/China exposure — zero tax
   friction across the board.
2. **Use Irish-domiciled ETFs** for US/global equity exposure — saves 15% on
   dividend withholding vs US-domiciled ETFs and eliminates estate tax risk.
3. **Always submit W-8BEN** to your broker when opening a US trading account —
   this is mandatory and must be renewed every 3 years.
4. **Review US-situs exposure** if total US-listed holdings exceed US$60,000 —
   consider migrating to Irish-domiciled equivalents or a holding structure.
5. **Prefer accumulating ETFs** over distributing ones for additional tax
   efficiency (no dividend distribution events at investor level).
6. **Document your holdings** by domicile and situs for estate planning —
   your executor needs to know what's US-situs and what isn't.

## Addendum (2026-09-01): HK-listed US trackers — check the domicile, not the venue

HKEX lists two economically similar but tax-opposite ways to track US indices:

- **Cross-listed US-domiciled funds** — e.g. **3455.HK** (Invesco QQQ Trust,
  cross-listed on HKEX Feb 2025; ISIN `US46090E…`). Buying in HKD during HK
  hours changes nothing tax-wise: **still US-situs (estate tax applies above
  US$60k) and still 30% dividend WHT**. Venue ≠ domicile.
- **HK-domiciled US-index trackers** — e.g. **3195.HK** (Hang Seng S&P 500,
  Hang Seng Investment Management, HKD counter). Not US-situs ⇒ **no US
  estate tax**. But HK has no US tax treaty, so US dividends are hit **30%
  WHT inside the fund** (vs 15% for Irish UCITS) — an embedded drag of ≈
  (yield × 15pp): ~0.08%/yr on QQQ-yield (~0.55%), ~0.2%/yr on a ~1.3%
  broad-market yield. ⚠️ Also compare **TERs per fund** — 3195.HK's is ~0.90%
  vs ~0.07% for CSPX; on low-yield indices the TER gap can exceed the WHT gap.

Project decision (2026-09-01): the agentic-trading platform dropped its
LSE/UCITS lane; tax-efficient US exposure uses the **HK-domiciled** trackers,
accepting the ~0.2%/yr drag for simplicity. Irish UCITS remains the
tax-optimal wrapper (15% + no estate tax) if a third venue is ever acceptable.
